Macs, October 6, 2026. The page for it, rendered from the same result files

## Where the time goes

- **CedarDB stalls.** Its first tic after the level starts takes 16.9 s on
  Capitola and 26.1 s on Morrobay, which looks like compiling the tic. Later
  tics take as long again, every 280 to 390 tic-and-frame pairs: three
  times in every test R run on both hosts (on Capitola at tics 219, 594 and
  984 in the median run, 53.2 s of 163.8 s measured; on Morrobay at 348, 625
  and 939, 81.3 s of 191.6 s) and once in each test T run on Morrobay (tic
  1,158 in the median run), with a few frames of over a second besides. The
  stalls fall at nearly the same tics in all three runs on a host (within 6
  tics on Capitola, 23 on Morrobay) but not at the same tics on the two hosts.
  What CedarDB does then is not established here, and its log records nothing
  at those times. Between stalls, CedarDB plays 10.1 (Capitola) and 10.5
  (Morrobay) tic-and-frame pairs a second, and in test T, Capitola runs 41.2
  tics a second, faster than Doom's 35.
- **CedarDB's tics are slower between frames:** 73.0 ms (p50) in test R
  against 23.5 ms in test T on Capitola, 49.3 against 27.3 ms on Morrobay.
- **Sail is steady and slower per call**: a tic takes 41.4 ms and a frame
  99.5 ms (p50) on Capitola with no call over a second after the first tic,
  which plans the tic query for the level (4.3 s). On Morrobay, an 18-core
  Xeon from 2017, a tic takes 77.4 ms and a frame 166.6 ms, with 15 calls
  over a second in test R's measured interval.
- **Frames are Sail's largest cost**, about 100 ms on Capitola against
  CedarDB's 29 ms. SQLDoom's own client plays on Sail at about 32 tics a
  second because it skips frames to keep the game on time (test P, not
  measured here); test R draws one for every tic.

## Exactness (test E)

Every Sail snapshot matches CedarDB's at every tic, on both hosts, in all
runs: 1,200 of 1,200, bit-identical after the single-precision rule SailDoom's
oracle uses (a `real` compared as its shortest float32 text). CedarDB agrees
with itself across runs and across the two hosts, snapshot for snapshot and
frame for frame.

| Host | Frames differing by libm last bits | Other differences |
|---|---|---|
| Capitola | 49, 1 to 273 pixels | tic 730, 17,443 pixels: renderer: the floor under the player drawn with the wrong flat (open bug; DDD.md) |
| Morrobay | 102, 1 to 273 pixels | tic 730, 17,443 pixels: renderer: the floor under the player drawn with the wrong flat (open bug; DDD.md) |

- **Tic 730 is a renderer bug in SailDoom**: the floor under the player is
  drawn with the wrong flat. It happens from CedarDB's own recorded state too,
  so the game is right and the frame is wrong. It is open, recorded in
  [DDD.md](../../DDD.md), and the reason both Sail results are inexact.
- **The other differences are the platform's math.** Sail runs the same code
  on the same data on both Macs and draws 89 frames differently on the two;
  CedarDB runs glibc in a Linux VM on both and draws them identically. Of the
  differing frames, 33 differ on both Macs (Apple's libm against glibc) and
  the rest on one Mac only, where Sail on the other Mac draws the frame
  exactly as CedarDB does. Each frame's pixels and cause are in
  `frame-differences.json`; the differing frames were replayed with
  `driver.py frames` and checked against the digests the timed runs recorded.

## How the runs went

- On Capitola a first attempt, without per-tic times in the result files,
  was set aside after one run of each system: it showed CedarDB's stalls, and
  the driver was changed to keep every call's time. All six runs reported
  were made after that.
- The Sail runs on Morrobay first failed at startup: SailDoom's backend read
  SQLDoom's SQL from a path that existed only on Capitola. Fixed
  (`b143150`); the three Sail runs were made after the fix, and Morrobay's
  CedarDB runs, which never used that path, stand.
- The driver first stored each tic's snapshot as text, which compared a
  `real` as CedarDB's shortest text against Sail's exact float32 value. The
  stored snapshots were re-expressed through the driver's current rule after
  the Capitola runs (each such file says so); timings and frame digests are as
  recorded.
